ZSA Moonlander vs Kinesis Advantage360: Which Is Better for People Who Type All Day?

September 2026
Prices and specs below are approximate and illustrative — verify on the manufacturer's site before buying. No sponsored placements; these are editorial opinions.

If you're a programmer shopping for a serious ergonomic keyboard, you will eventually narrow it down to these two: the ZSA Moonlander (~$365) and the Kinesis Advantage360 (~$459–549). They're the two most-discussed split boards in the ergo community, and they represent two different philosophies. Here's the honest breakdown.

Layout Philosophy

The Moonlander is a flat columnar-stagger board: 72 keys in straight columns, with a 4-key thumb cluster per side. The Advantage360 is a concave keywell board: your fingers rest in sculpted bowls, so every key is roughly the same short distance away. Keywells are more radical and, for many people, more comfortable — but they're also a bigger departure from anything you've typed on before.

For programmers specifically, the keywell shape shines on long symbol-heavy sessions: brackets, parens, and operators live on layers, and shorter finger travel adds up over thousands of keystrokes a day.

Thumb Clusters: The Real Battleground

Programmers live on modifiers — Ctrl, Alt, Shift, and layer keys. The Advantage360's thumb clusters are widely considered the best in the business: large, well-positioned, easy to hit without thinking. The Moonlander's cluster is good but smaller, and some users find the outermost thumb keys a stretch.

If your workflow is Emacs-style chording or heavy tmux prefix use, this alone may decide it for you.

Software: Oryx vs ZMK

  • Moonlander (Oryx): a polished web configurator. Point, click, flash. You can drop into raw QMK if you want, but most programmers will never need to. Fastest path from box to productive.
  • Advantage360 (ZMK): open-source firmware, Bluetooth-first, configured via text files. More powerful (combos, mod-morphs, sticky keys done right) and more of a learning curve. If you enjoy configuring your tools as code, you'll love it; if not, budget an evening.

Portability and Tenting

The Moonlander folds nearly flat and travels well — it fits in a laptop bag. The Advantage360 is bulkier and stays on your desk. Both tent, but the Moonlander's folding legs are included and dead simple, while the 360's tenting is adjustable and more fiddly.

Price

Moonlander ~$365; Advantage360 ~$459 for the wired Signature, ~$549 for the Bluetooth Pro. The 360 costs more and asks more of you. Whether it's worth it depends on how much you value the keywells and the thumb cluster.

The Verdict

  • Get the Moonlander if this is your first serious ergo board, you travel, or you want the shortest path to productive.
  • Get the Advantage360 if you're already sold on ergonomics, you chord modifiers constantly, and you want the endgame board.

Both appear on our current top picks. Neither is a bad choice — they're just different answers to the same question.
