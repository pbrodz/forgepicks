Wildcat Lake Mini PCs Are $400. The $199 Box Still Wins

Published October 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Intel's Wildcat Lake mini PCs are here, and the cheapest ones land around $380–$390: the GMKtec NucBox M3S at $389.99, the Bosgame E6 ECO at $379.99, and the Peladn WO5 at about $387. That is roughly twice the price of the Beelink EQ12, our $199 default pick. So the obvious question: does the new silicon beat the old box for a home server? Short answer: no. Not for this job.

What $390 buys you

Every cheap Wildcat Lake box is built around the same entry-level chip, Intel's Core 3 304: five cores (one performance, four efficiency), boosting to 4.3GHz, with a neural processor rated at 15 TOPS. The GMKtec M3S pairs it with 12GB of soldered LPDDR5 memory, a 256GB or 512GB SSD plus a second M.2 slot, USB4, dual HDMI, single 2.5GbE, and Windows 11 Pro. The Bosgame and Peladn versions rhyme: same chip, same soldered 12GB, similar ports. The pricier Wildcat boxes — HP's OmniDesk from $500, Beelink's EQi from $509, MINIX's N304 at $715 — are a different tier entirely, and none of them is the $400 box in this post's title.

What $199 buys you

The EQ12 runs Intel's N100: four cores, 6W TDP, 16GB of DDR5, a 512GB NVMe drive you can expand, and — the part that matters most for a server — dual 2.5GbE ports. It idles around 7–10W at the wall and handles a dozen Docker containers without breaking a sweat. Against the Wildcat boxes, that is more RAM (16GB vs 12GB), a second fast network port, and storage you can actually grow. Those are the three things a home server cares about, and the cheaper box wins all three.

Where Wildcat genuinely wins

Fair is fair: the new chip has real advantages. The 15 TOPS neural processor is the first reason to care — if you want to experiment with local AI on the box itself, the N100 has nothing comparable. USB4 is the second: 40Gbps, and on the Peladn it even brings eGPU support. And it is a newer platform, so it will likely get driver and firmware attention longer. If your workload is local AI or fast peripherals, those are honest reasons to pay more.

The catch that matters: soldered RAM

But here is the catch that decides it for a home server. Every $390 Wildcat box solders its 12GB to the board. You cannot upgrade it later. A home server grows: more containers, more services, one more thing running in the closet. Buying a box with a fixed 12GB ceiling, for twice the price of a box with 16GB and room to grow, is the wrong trade — especially when the Wildcat box also drops to a single 2.5GbE port, giving up the EQ12's router and NAS flexibility. New silicon does not fix a worse memory and networking deal.

Verdict

Buy the $199 box. For Docker, Plex, Pi-hole, and the usual closet services, the EQ12 at ~$199 remains the right answer, and the first Wildcat Lake boxes at ~$390 do not change that. Pay the Wildcat premium only if you specifically want the neural processor or USB4 — and if you do, go in knowing the RAM is soldered and the second network port is gone. We are watching Wildcat Lake, not adding it to the list: when a Wildcat box shows up with upgradeable RAM at a price that respects the EQ12, that is a different conversation.
