Beelink EQ14 vs GMKtec G3 Plus: Which Is the Better Home Server?

Published September 28, 2026 · 5 min read

The Beelink EQ14 and the GMKtec G3 Plus are the two most obvious Intel N150 mini PCs on the market right now — same chip, same 6W envelope, same dual 2.5GbE pitch to homelab buyers. So the question isn't which is faster. It's whether the EQ14's price premium buys you anything at all.

They're the same computer, mostly

Let's be blunt: the silicon is identical, the memory and storage configs overlap, and both give you the dual 2.5GbE ports that matter for server duty (one for LAN, one for a direct NAS link, or a DIY router setup). Any performance difference between these two boxes in Docker or Plex will come down to cooling and firmware, not the badge — and both are 6W chips that barely break a sweat.

Where they actually differ

Price

The G3 Plus typically lists $15–25 under the EQ14. For a home server that will sit in a closet running containers, that gap buys you nothing in capability — it's pure savings.

Ports and chassis

Check the exact port layout before buying: USB count, HDMI vs DisplayPort, and whether there's a 2.5" SATA bay for a bulk HDD vary between revisions. If you plan to attach external storage, the box with the ports you need wins regardless of brand.

Vendor and support

Beelink has a longer track record and a more active user community; GMKtec support is thinner. If this is your first homelab box and you want hand-holding, that's worth something. If you've flashed a BIOS before, it isn't.

Verdict

Buy the GMKtec G3 Plus unless the EQ14 is on sale at parity or you specifically want Beelink's support. Same chip, same RAM, same networking — the cheaper box wins. See where both sit on our top picks page.
