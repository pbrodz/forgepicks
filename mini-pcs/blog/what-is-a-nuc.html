What is a NUC, and why is there none on our shortlist?

Published October 23, 2026 · 6 min read

Every mini PC conversation eventually name-drops the NUC - and readers reasonably ask whether the boxes on our shortlist are NUCs, and why none of them carries the name. Short answer: NUC is a brand that became a category, the brand changed hands, and the current boxes keep losing on the rules this site buys by. Longer answer below.

Honesty box: we have nothing against the badge - a NUC that wins on value and upgradability will be listed here the day one does. This post explains the scoring, not a grudge.

What NUC actually is

NUC stands for Next Unit of Computing - Intel's mini PC line, launched in 2013. The formula was radical at the time: a real desktop processor on a 4x4-inch board, in a box the size of a paperback, sold mostly as a barebone kit - you added your own RAM, SSD, and operating system. For a decade it was the default answer: home-theater PCs, office desktops, and a generation of homelabs (Proxmox clusters of three NUCs were practically a uniform) ran on them.

The name escaped the brand. Like Kleenex for tissues, "a NUC" now means any small mini PC in most forum conversations - which is why the word is everywhere even though actual NUCs are a shrinking slice of what people buy. Most "NUCs" in a 2026 homelab thread are Beelink, GMKtec, or Minisforum boxes wearing the nickname.

Then Intel left, and ASUS took over

In 2023 Intel discontinued the NUC line and handed manufacturing, sales, and support to ASUS. Today's genuine NUCs are ASUS NUCs: the NUC 14 Pro and 15 Pro for mainstream buyers, the Pro AI models with laptop-class AI chips, and the ROG NUC - a gaming variant with a discrete GPU at a distinctly un-mini price. Intel-era models are used-market only now.

So why is there none on the list?

Three reasons, all boring, all decisive:

  • The Intel ones are discontinued. Our shortlist only lists machines you can buy new, with a warranty, today.
  • The ASUS ones cost more for the same silicon. The NUC badge carries a premium, and this site's readers are buying home servers, not fleet desktops. When a NUC 14 Pro AI sells at $1,000 with soldered RAM while a socketed competitor sits below it, the competitor gets the slot.
  • The AI line fails our first rule. Our top buying rule is socketed RAM at real money (see the tiers explainer). The current AI NUCs solder it to a laptop processor. Fine machines; wrong trade at that price.

And the fair counterpoint, because there is one: if you value warranty, support, and validated build quality over price-per-spec, a NUC is a rational buy - arguably the rational buy for a small business. Three years of ASUS support and boring consistency are worth money. Our list simply optimizes for the home buyer who'd rather have the sockets and the savings.

The bottom line

NUC is the name that built the category, now kept by ASUS, and used by everyone to mean everything. Buy the box that wins your checklist - RAM sockets, storage slots, ports, idle watts - and let the badge be a tiebreaker, not the decision. The day an ASUS NUC tops the value chart, it will appear on our shortlist like anything else: on merit.
