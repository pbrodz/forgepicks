RAM Prices Are Rising: What Mini PC Buyers Should Know (2026)

Published October 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Are mini PCs about to get more expensive? The past two weeks brought the clearest sign yet that the answer is yes — at least for the RAM-heavy ones. Corsair confirmed higher prices across its Strix Halo AI workstations, Acer warned of PC price increases around 20%, and the Raspberry Pi 5's memory-driven price creep continued. Here's what's happening, and what it means if you're building a home server.

Honesty box: prices below are figures reported Oct 1–14, 2026 from vendors and tech press — they move constantly, so verify before buying. Where we haven't verified a number ourselves, we say so.

What happened

Corsair confirmed new pricing for its AI Workstation 300 (Ryzen AI Max "Strix Halo") mini PCs around Oct 5, per Notebookcheck: the base Ryzen AI Max 385 / 64GB model went from $1,599 to $1,699 (+6.25%), and the Ryzen AI Max+ 395 / 128GB SKUs jumped 35–48%, with the flagship now at $3,399.99 (TweakTown). Corsair gave no explanation, but the pattern points at memory and storage costs.

Elsewhere: Acer reportedly warned of PC price increases around 20% tied to the memory crunch, and the Raspberry Pi 5 2GB reportedly rose again on Oct 1, from $65 to $77.50 — the fourth memory-driven hike this year, per price-tracking reports.

Why memory, why now

AI data centers are vacuuming up the world's DRAM supply, and everyone downstream pays. We covered the Pi 5's side of this on Oct 1: the 8GB board went from $80 to $175 MSRP, and the hikes landed hardest on the big-RAM configs (the 16GB board is up 154% since launch; the 1GB and 2GB boards barely moved). That's the tell — the cost sits in the memory, not the silicon. Raspberry Pi's CEO has been blunt that these are pass-through costs, not profiteering. "Temporary" has now meant the better part of a year.

The latest data points the same way. Per Tom’s Hardware tracking (via Zubiqo, Oct 14), the cheapest 32GB DDR5-6000 kit hit $449.97, up from $400 in August. Barclays’ October data has DDR5 spot prices up 8% month-on-month, DDR4 contract prices up 24% over three months, and Micron’s supply constrained through 2028; Citi puts the DRAM shortage’s peak in Q4 2027. These are reported figures, not prices we checked ourselves — but the direction is the same everywhere: up, and not soon reversing.

What it means for your home server

For typical home-server duty — Docker, Plex, Pi-hole, file serving — the budget picks haven't moved. Our top pick, the Beelink EQ12, is still ~$199–205 with 16GB of RAM and a 512GB SSD in the box. These boxes ship with modest memory configs, so the DRAM crunch hasn't reached their price tags yet.

Where it has landed is the big-RAM class: 64–192GB Strix Halo boxes built for local LLMs. If you've been eyeing one of those, the price creep is already real — the price war we covered on Oct 5 is now fighting against rising memory costs.

Practical take: don't panic-buy a budget box — buy on your own schedule; no hike has landed there yet. But if a high-RAM mini PC is on your list, sooner likely beats later, because the increases are flowing one direction.

Bottom line

Should you buy now? If it's a budget N100/N150 box, buy when you're ready — the memory crisis hasn't touched that shelf yet. If it's a big-RAM AI box, waiting is likely to cost you. And keep an eye on our Fresh from the forge box — if the hikes reach the budget end, that's where we'll note it first.
