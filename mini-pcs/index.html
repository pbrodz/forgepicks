Best overall

Beelink EQ12 (N100) ~$199

“One box to serve them all.”

The default answer. The N100 sips power at idle, handles a dozen Docker containers without breaking a sweat, and the EQ12's dual 2.5GbE ports make it a natural fit for router or NAS duty. If you buy one box and stop thinking about it, buy this one.

  • CPU: Intel N100, 4C/4T, 6W TDP
  • RAM: 16GB DDR5 (typical config)
  • Storage: 512GB NVMe, expandable
  • Net: 2× 2.5GbE
  • Idle draw: widely reported ~7–10W at the wall*
  • Price: ~$199

Good

  • Excellent performance per watt
  • Dual 2.5GbE — rare at this price
  • Silent under server loads

Watch out

  • Single-channel RAM on some configs
  • Weak for 4K Plex transcodes

*Illustrative range based on reviewer reports, not our own measurement.

“A small step up, if the price is right.”

  • CPU: Intel N150, 4C/4T, 6W TDP

  • Storage: 512GB NVMe
  • Net: Dual 1GbE (1000M) LAN
  • Price: ~$190

Good

  • Newest low-power Intel chip

Watch out

  • Small premium over N100 for small gains

Power pick (barebone)

Minisforum UM890 Pro (Ryzen 9 8945HS) $463 barebone

“Bring your own RAM — the muscle is included.”

The UM690S’s successor takes the power-pick slot — and the honest version of it. The 8945HS is an 8-core/16-thread Hawk Point chip with a Radeon 780M, dual 2.5GbE, OCuLink, and dual USB4, in a box built for VMs, game servers, and Plex transcodes the N-series boxes can’t touch. The catch is the configuration: Minisforum sells it barebone, so you supply two DDR5 SODIMMs (up to 96GB), your M.2 SSDs, and an OS. At today’s RAM prices, budget for that — the $463 buys the muscle, not the memory. A 32GB DDR5 SODIMM kit runs roughly $360–560 right now and a 1TB Gen4 SSD about $190, so building one from barebone lands around $1,000–1,225 all-in — more than the GMKtec K11 complete at ~$940. Barebone earns its card for buyers who already own DDR5 SODIMM, an M.2 SSD, and an OS licence; if you’re buying everything new, the K11 is the cheaper route to the same chip. A configured 32GB/512GB version is in stock at $919; the 32GB/1TB version is $991 and out of stock. A 70W performance mode and active DDR/SSD cooling keep it fed under load.

  • CPU: AMD Ryzen 9 8945HS, 8C/16T, up to 5.2GHz
  • RAM: Barebone — 2× DDR5 SODIMM, up to 96GB (you supply)
  • Storage: Barebone — 2× M.2 PCIe 4.0 (you supply)
  • Net: 2× 2.5GbE, Wi-Fi 6E
  • Price: $463 barebone (no RAM / SSD / OS)

Good

  • 8C/16T Hawk Point muscle for VMs and heavy transcodes
  • Dual 2.5GbE + OCuLink + dual USB4
  • 96GB RAM ceiling, dual M.2 slots
  • 70W mode with active DDR/SSD cooling
  • Cheapest entry to an 8945HS box — if you already own RAM/SSD

Watch out

  • Barebone — no RAM, SSD, or OS included
  • All-in ~$1,000–1,225 with new RAM + SSD — more than a complete 8945HS box (~$940)
  • Idle power not yet verified
  • Configured 32GB/1TB is $991 and out of stock (32GB/512GB in stock at $919)

Power pick

GMKtec K11 (Ryzen 9 8945HS) ~$940

“Desktop money, mini-PC footprint.”

This is the card that makes you wince: $940 for a mini PC is full-desktop money. But it’s the honest price of a complete 8945HS box in October 2026 — and the cheapest way to a working one. You get 32GB DDR5-5600, a 1TB PCIe 4.0 SSD, and Windows 11 Pro in the box: no parts hunt, no assembly, no licence to source. Same chip as the UM890 Pro, with dual 2.5GbE, OCuLink, and dual USB4 for the homelab roles the N-series boxes can’t touch. Against the M6, this is the box for x86, Windows, dual LAN, and OCuLink. And if you’re buying everything new, it’s cheaper all-in than the UM890 Pro plus parts.

  • CPU: AMD Ryzen 9 8945HS, 8C/16T, up to 5.2GHz
  • RAM: 32GB DDR5-5600
  • Storage: 1TB PCIe 4.0 NVMe (dual M.2, up to 8TB)
  • Net: 2× 2.5GbE, Wi-Fi 6
  • Price: ~$940 (Amazon; ~$893 after 5% Prime Savings at checkout)

Good

  • Complete — RAM, SSD, Windows 11 Pro included
  • Same 8945HS/780M muscle as the UM890 Pro
  • Dual 2.5GbE + OCuLink + dual USB4

Watch out

Previous generation

Apple Mac Mini (M4) Discontinued

Photo: Seasider53, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

“The one that isn’t supposed to be here.”

Yes, it’s the odd one out — ARM instead of x86, Apple instead of Beelink, and roughly triple the price of our budget pick. But the M4 Mini idles around 4W, runs silent, and is a genuinely superb little server: Plex with hardware transcoding, a stack of Docker containers (most mainstream images ship ARM builds now, though a few stragglers still need x86 emulation), and — the reason people kept asking — a wonderful always-on host for OpenClaw and other local AI agents, with 16GB of fast unified memory to feed them. The caveats are real: macOS is no longer a server OS, so forget bare-metal Proxmox; the base model ships with plain gigabit Ethernet (10GbE is a paid upgrade); and the RAM and storage are soldered, so buy the configuration you’ll want in three years. Apple’s newer M6 Mini (from $899) now sits above it — see the card below — and Apple discontinued the M4 Mini in August 2026, so the M4 is now remaining-stock only at third-party retailers. Buy the M6 unless you find a refurbished M4 well under $599.

  • CPU: Apple M4, 10-core CPU / 10-core GPU
  • RAM: 16GB unified (up to 32GB)
  • Storage: 256GB SSD (soldered)
  • Net: 1× Gigabit Ethernet (10GbE optional)
  • Idle draw: widely reported ~4W at the wall*
  • Price: Discontinued at Apple (August 2026); remaining stock only

Good

  • Sips power and runs silent, 24/7
  • Superb host for OpenClaw and local AI agents
  • Excellent Plex box with hardware transcoding
  • 16GB of fast unified memory as standard

Watch out

  • ARM — some Docker images need x86 emulation
  • macOS isn’t a server OS; no bare-metal Proxmox
  • Gigabit Ethernet standard; 10GbE costs extra
  • RAM and storage are soldered, not upgradeable

Premium pick

Apple Mac Mini (M6) ~$779.99

Photo: Seasider53, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

“The premium pick, rebuilt for the AI age.”

Everything the M4 card says, turned up a notch. The M6 is Apple’s first 2 nm Mac chip — 12-core CPU, 12-core GPU with neural accelerators in every core, and a dual 16-core Neural Engine — and reviewers call it the fastest single-core Mac ever made, built openly for on-device AI: local models, creative AI apps, agent workloads. The old complaints are mostly fixed: 2.5GbE Ethernet is now standard, with Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6 aboard. The remaining caveats are the Apple ones: macOS is not a server OS (so no bare-metal Proxmox), ARM means a few Docker images still need x86 emulation, and RAM and storage are soldered — with the M6 topping out at 32GB of unified memory, so truly large local models will want the M5 Pro config ($1,699, up to 64GB). At $779.99 on Amazon (an all-time low at press time, $120 off list), it’s roughly $180 over the M4 for a genuinely meaningful jump for local-AI work.

  • CPU: Apple M6, 12-core CPU / 12-core GPU (2 nm)
  • RAM: 16GB unified (up to 32GB)
  • Storage: 256GB SSD (soldered)
  • Net: 1× 2.5GbE Ethernet (standard)
  • Idle draw: Apple-silicon class — M4 widely reported ~4W at the wall*
  • Price: ~$779.99 (all-time low, Amazon)

Good

  • Fastest single-core of any Mac, per reviewers

Lowest idle power

Raspberry Pi 5 ~$200

Photo: Anil Öztas, via Wikimedia Commons

“The little board that could.”

Don’t let the credit-card size fool you — the Pi 5 is a legitimate always-on server, and it’s the one I run myself. It idles around 3W, runs silent, and the community has already solved every problem you’ll hit: Pi-hole, Home Assistant, and a stack of Docker containers all hum along. The honest math: the $200 board needs a power supply, case, and storage, so budget roughly $250 all-in (up after the 2026 RAM price hikes) — and it’s ARM, so the odd Docker image still lacks a build, and you get plain gigabit Ethernet rather than 2.5GbE. But per watt, nothing on this page touches it.

  • CPU: Broadcom BCM2712, 4× Cortex-A76 @ 2.4GHz
  • RAM: 8GB LPDDR4X (4GB and 16GB options)
  • Storage: microSD (NVMe via PCIe HAT, extra)
  • Net: 1× Gigabit Ethernet, Wi-Fi 5
  • Idle draw: widely reported ~3W at the wall*
  • Price: ~$200 board (~$250 kitted out)

Good

  • ~3W idle — cheapest box here to run 24/7
  • Enormous community; everything just works
  • GPIO and HATs for hardware projects
  • Silent and tiny enough to hide anywhere

Watch out

  • ARM — some Docker images lack builds
  • Gigabit Ethernet only, no 2.5GbE
  • Real cost is ~$250+ once kitted out
  • microSD wear (or pay extra for NVMe)

*Illustrative figure based on reviewer reports, not our own measurement.

How we pick

For a home server, the ranking criteria are simple, in order: idle power draw (it runs 24/7), networking (dual 2.5GbE is the sweet spot), price, then raw CPU speed. A box that idles at 8W costs roughly $10–15 a year to run (illustrative, at typical US residential rates) — a box idling at 30W costs three to four times that, every year, forever.
